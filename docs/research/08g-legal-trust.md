# 08g: Legal and trust review for "Student Loan Copilot"

Research date: 2026-11-14. Author role: consumer-finance compliance researcher. **This is not legal advice.** It summarizes laws and cases with citations so that a licensed attorney can review the conclusions quickly. Have counsel in CA, NY and WA sign off before charging residents of those states.

**Product assumed:** a B2C web app with no login, priced at $79/yr or $9/mo. Users enter their own data or upload their own files. It (a) recommends the cheapest federal repayment plan, (b) gives a dated action plan and letter templates the user sends, and (c) checks servicer statements for errors. It never contacts servicers, never files applications, and never asks for FSA ID credentials.

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **Charging for software that gives plan-selection guidance and self-help tools is lawful at the federal level.** That holds if the product is sold online without telemarketing and does not deceive. No federal statute bans charging for help that ED also gives for free. The cases below punished deception, impersonation, advance fees taken through **telemarketing**, FSA ID takeover and diverted payments, not the act of charging. Charging for something that is free elsewhere, without saying so, is the core "fee harvesting" theory (CFPB, *Student Loan Pro*, 2024). The fix is a prominent "it's free at StudentAid.gov" disclosure.
2. **The real blockers are state laws, not federal ones:**
   - **New York (Financial Services Law Art. 7, §§701–706, V):** we are almost certainly a "student debt consultant". The definition expressly includes services represented to "advise the borrower which student loan repayment plan or forgiveness program to consider" and to "educate the borrower about student loan repayment". NY **bans taking any payment "before the full completion of all such services"**. It also requires a written contract **acknowledged by a NY notary**, a 5-business-day cancellation right, and 12-pt bold ad disclosures. Penalties are up to $10k per violation, plus a private right of action with treble damages for intentional violations. **An upfront $79/yr or $9/mo subscription does not fit this law.** Exclude NY residents from paid plans (or make the product free for them) until NY counsel advises otherwise.
   - **California (CCFPL registration rules, 10 CCR §§1000–1043, operative 2025-02-15, V via LII):** "student debt relief services" include "**assessing suitability or providing advice**" and "**preparing documents to be submitted by a consumer**" in connection with an attempt to secure "a revised payment plan … consolidation, or forgiveness". Only tax-exempt nonprofits that serve consumers **free of charge** are exempt. **We must register with DFPI (through NMLS) before charging California residents, or block California.** Once registered, the website must state the DFPI registration and the registration number (10 CCR §1012(b)). An annual report on SDR metrics is also required (§1043).
   - **Washington (S/U):** the debt-adjusting statute, RCW 18.28, literally covers "counseling … of the indebtedness of a debtor" for compensation and caps fees ($25 initial charge, 15% of debt). It was written for debt-management plans, but its literal wording reaches us. Get a WA counsel opinion or a DFI interpretive letter, or exclude WA paid users.
3. **CFPB:** we are probably a "covered person". Individualized plan advice fits "financial advisory services … to assist a consumer with debt management … modifying the terms of any extension of credit" (12 U.S.C. §5481(15)(A)(viii)(II), V). CFPB enforcement has been nearly dormant since early 2025 (V: the CFPB enforcement page lists only one new action after January 2025). **State AGs can still enforce the CFPA's UDAAP ban** (12 U.S.C. §5552, V).
4. **GLBA:** treat ourselves as a "financial institution". The FTC Safeguards Rule lists "credit counseling service" and "investment advisory company" as examples. Under Reg P, "obtains financial … advisory services from you for a fee" creates a customer relationship (V). We therefore need a written information-security program, a privacy notice, and the FTC breach-notice process.
5. **TSR:** the advance-fee ban (16 CFR §310.4(a)(5)) applies **only to telemarketing**. The definition still requires "use of one or more telephones" and "more than one interstate telephone call" (V; current text, last amended 89 FR 99075, 2024-12-10). Neither 2024 amendment changed that. **The moment we sell by phone, including inbound calls from our ads, the ban applies.** Debt relief is carved out of the inbound-call exemptions (§310.6(b)(5)(i), (b)(6)(i), V). Rule: no phone sales, ever.

**On question 1, "can a private company legally charge?":** Yes. No federal statute or rule prohibits a for-profit company from charging for repayment-plan guidance or self-help tools. Federal limits on *how* we charge apply only through the TSR (telemarketing), CROA (credit-repair claims) and ROSCA (subscriptions). Federal limits on *what we say* come from §5, Part 461 and CFPA UDAAP. State law decides whether we can charge **upfront** (NY: no) and whether we must **register** first (CA: yes).

**Patterns across these cases (what to never resemble):**
1. **Telephone selling** with advance fees. This is the TSR hook in almost every case.
2. **Impersonating ED or servicers**, or using names that suggest a federal program ("Federal Direct Group", "Biden Loan Forgiveness", "Student Loan Relief Department").
3. **Taking FSA IDs** and changing contact information or passwords (*Mission Hills*, *Arete*, *USASDR*).
4. **Diverting loan payments**: "we'll take over servicing", "your fees count toward your loan".
5. **Promising or guaranteeing forgiveness** or a specific low payment.
6. **Charging to "enroll" in free ED programs** (*Student Loan Pro*; *Superior Servicing*: "at most … filled out free ED applications").
7. **Fake reviews**, foreign-language bait-and-switch, and financing fees with undisclosed loans.

**What none of these cases attacked:** a disclosed, web-only, self-serve tool that tells users the applications are free and has them act themselves. We found no enforcement action against that model. That is an absence of evidence, not a safe harbor.

## 5. Do's and don'ts

### Never do

1. **Never ask for, accept, store or use an FSA ID** (username or password), and never log into StudentAid.gov or a servicer portal as the user. This is an explicit NY prohibition (§702(e)) and was central to *Mission Hills*, *Arete* and *USASDR*. Tell users never to share it.
2. **Never sell by telephone.** No outbound sales calls, no "call to sign up" numbers, no taking orders on support calls, no upselling by phone. Any of these turns us into "telemarketing" a "debt relief service" and makes the subscription an illegal advance fee (16 CFR §310.4(a)(5); §310.6(b)(5)(i)).
3. **Never charge to "enroll", "apply", "consolidate", "file" or "process"** anything with ED or a servicer, and never sell a "document preparation" service for ED forms (*Student Loan Pro*; *Superior Servicing*; Doc Prep cases).
4. **Never promise or imply forgiveness, approval, or a specific payment or savings amount.** Show estimates with their assumptions and say they are not guaranteed. No "eliminate your loans", "$0 payments guaranteed" or "approved".
5. **Never imply government affiliation.** No ED or FSA seals or logos, no eagle or flag motifs styled as official, no ".gov"-looking domains, no "Federal", "National", "U.S.", "Department", "Program", "Biden/Trump Forgiveness" or "StudentAid" in the brand, ads or sender names. Do not use "official", "approved", "authorized" or "certified by ED". No ads that bid on, or mimic, "studentaid.gov login" (16 CFR §461.2; NY §702(g); 18 U.S.C. §709).
6. **Never call ourselves a "servicer"**, say we "take over" or "manage" the loan, or accept loan payments. **Never imply our fee goes toward the loan** (NY §702(g)(2); SL Finance; Superior).
7. **Never tell users to stop paying their servicer** (*Superior Servicing*).
8. **Never take a power of attorney or third-party authorization** to act with the servicer, and never keep original loan documents (NY §702(c)–(d)).
9. **Never say or imply users can't do it themselves** (NY §702(f)). The whole pitch is "you do it; we make it faster and clearer".
10. **Never market credit-score improvement or "credit repair"** (CROA).
11. **Never robocall or text without prior express written consent** captured separately from checkout, and never buy "student loan forgiveness" leads (TCPA; NERD Solutions and SL Finance DNC counts).
12. **Never post fake, incentivized-without-disclosure, or AI-generated reviews or testimonials** (16 CFR Part 465; USASDR).
13. **Never sell or share uploaded statements or user data** with lenders, refinancers or lead buyers without GLBA-compliant notice and opt-out. The default is no sharing.
14. **Never take NY residents' money upfront** under the current model, and **never charge CA residents before DFPI registration**.
15. **Never claim "AI lawyer" or lawyer-level accuracy** (DoNotPay).
16. **Never use foreign-language marketing with English-only terms** (USASDR).

### Do

1. **Web-only checkout.** Support by email or chat only, or a phone line that never sells.
2. **Put the "free at StudentAid.gov or through your servicer" disclosure** on the landing page, results page, paywall and every template, with a link. This is the main mitigation for CFPA "abusive" and §5 deception risk.
3. **Adopt the NY §703 five-point disclosure everywhere** (ads, landing page, paywall) in ≥12-pt bold. It costs little and covers the strictest state.
4. **Geo-gate:** detect state at checkout (billing ZIP plus a self-attestation). **NY:** free tier only, or no sale, until counsel signs off. **CA:** register with DFPI first, then show the registration number in the footer. **WA:** hold until counsel advises.
5. **ROSCA and auto-renewal hygiene:** state the price, billing period, auto-renewal and how to cancel **before** card entry. Use an unchecked consent box. Send a confirmation email. Offer **online one-click cancellation**. Send a reminder 7–30 days before the annual renewal. Consider a 14-day no-questions refund, which also helps answer "fee before services complete" concerns.
6. **GLBA program:** a written information-security program; a named Qualified Individual; risk assessment; encryption in transit and at rest; MFA for admin access; vendor contracts; data-retention limits (delete uploads after analysis, or parse client-side); an incident-response plan; an FTC breach notice process (≥500 consumers, 30 days). Publish a GLBA privacy notice.
7. **Data minimization:** do not collect full SSNs. Redact account numbers from uploads. Prefer in-browser parsing.
8. **Substantiation file:** for every savings or "errors found" claim, keep the method, test set, assumptions and expert review. Date-stamp the rules engine version (see 08a: post-P.L. 119-21 rules, RISE final rule effective 2026-07-01).
9. **Template review:** attorney or expert review; factual tone; the Texas-style "not a substitute for an attorney" line; refer to the FSA Ombudsman and legal aid for disputes.
10. **Brand and visual identity** that is clearly private: company name and address in the footer, "independent" wording, non-governmental colors and typography.
11. **Truthful comparison to free tools:** if you mention StudentAid.gov's Loan Simulator, do not disparage it falsely.
12. **Log consent and disclosures shown** (screenshots and versioned copy) for each checkout. This is the evidence in any AG inquiry.
